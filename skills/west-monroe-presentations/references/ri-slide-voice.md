# Ri's slide voice

Apply this voice to every deck by default, together with the [West Monroe brand voice](brand-voice.md). Write personal, direct copy: a capable practitioner explaining to the reader, across a table, what happens, who does it and why it matters to them. Never write marketing copy. Do not write humor or jokes.

## Every line does work

- Cut lines that are too clever: "Steering is the work."
- Cut setup lines: "Steering is the challenge." followed by the sentence that explains it. Keep the explaining sentence.
- Cut self-description: "We are fully committed to your success." "We want to be transparent about where things stand."

Compress freely, but every compressed line must still name **an actor, a mechanism, a number or the reader's situation**. Cut any line that trades something concrete for something abstract or exists only for rhythm.

| Write | Don't write |
| --- | --- |
| "**Two worker pools.** Heavy runs binary-dependent jobs; light runs everything else." | "Get it wrong and you starve the pool that matters." |
| "Brokers submit once; underwriters stop re-keying." | "One intake. Zero friction." |
| "Three intake errors cause 60% of rework." | "Rework is an intake problem." |

When copy does not fit, put fewer ideas on the slide or break the idea into smaller chunks: bolded-lead bullets, a table, a diagram, a second slide. Shorten sentences; keep the reasoning.

## Personal and direct

| Write | Don't write |
| --- | --- |
| "Your adjusters re-enter the same claim data in three systems." | "Fragmented systems create inefficiencies across the enterprise." |
| "We'd start with the two plants that miss the most shipments." | "A phased approach to unlock value at scale." |
| "This will be slower for the first two sprints. After that, the team stops re-testing by hand." | "Accelerate delivery with next-generation automation." |
| "Your team keeps the vendor relationship; we take the day-to-day integration work." | "A trusted partnership model that empowers your organization." |

Speak to "you" and "your team", name real roles and systems, admit a cost where there is one, and say what changes in the reader's week.

## Titles

- Sentence case, and a full sentence stating the page's conclusion. Read the titles alone, in order; they must tell the whole story.
- "Undocumented stored procedures, not data volume, will set the migration timeline." Not "Migration considerations."
- Causal and conditional titles are good: "If finance and operations share one data layer, month-end close drops from eight days to five."
- One line where possible, two at most. A three-line title is carrying body copy.

## Sentences

- **Make the logic explicit** with "because", "so", "if… then", "instead of", "on the other hand". Don't set two fragments side by side and leave the reader to connect them.
- **Repeat the key term.** Say "intake errors" every time; don't vary it to "submission issues" or "front-end defects".
- **Name the referent.** No bare "this" or "it" when the reader could wonder what it means.
- **Second person.** "You", "your team". Never "your people", "resources" or "organizations face…".
- **Hedge the inference, assert the principle.** Mark a judgment once ("We think", "likely", "in our experience"). Never stack hedges. Vary the hedge wording across a deck.
- **Precise technical terms.** "GitLab is self-hosted and runs our CI/CD pipelines", not "GitLab is where the code actually runs".
- **Numbers, intervals and dates.** "Two to three times as long", "by March 14". Not "significantly" or "soon".
- **"Not X, but Y" only when the reader actually believes X.** "We don't just advise; we work alongside your team" works because clients assume consultants only advise. "Not just a tool, but a philosophy" doesn't.
- **Name who decided.** "Your CFO set the June deadline", not "The deadline is June".

## Persuasion and proof

- **Competitor test.** If another consulting firm could sign the sentence unchanged, rewrite it.
- **Attach proof matched to the reader:** a count, their industry segment and, when approved, a named client. "[N] implementations for regional health plans since [year], including [client]." Not "a proven track record with organizations like yours."
- **Make differentiators contestable:** something a competitor might decline to claim, followed by one or two specific proof points.
- **Name the obstacle.** If the work is hard, say why.
- **Missing proof:** write the specific claim with a visible placeholder and add an open question. Don't soften it into a generic claim.

## Signature moves

Use these where they fit; don't force all of them into every deck.

1. **Name the reader's objection and answer it.** "Your finance team may worry that moving reports off Excel means losing the adjustments they make by hand. The new model keeps a governed adjustment step, so those adjustments are recorded instead of lost." Use a callout, a "What you might be thinking" row, or an objection-and-answer pair.
2. **Concede, then redirect.** Give ground on what is true, then state what matters: *what you don't need to do* / *what you need to do*. "You still own the vendor relationship, but you no longer manage the integration day to day."
3. **Argue by asymmetry.** Put the insight in the gap: before and after, effort and value, then and now. "Your team spends 70% of close reconciling and 30% analyzing; we want to flip that ratio." Use a personal dated interval ("on a project 2.5 years ago…") only from a real anecdote.
4. **Ground the abstraction.** Say where it lives, who uses it, in what situation. "Governance" becomes "the weekly change-review meeting where your architects approve schema changes". Keep an explanatory model intact; shorten its sentences instead.
5. **Enumerate the real mechanisms.** "Rework comes from three places: missing member IDs, outdated provider addresses and duplicate submissions."
6. **Use three only when there are three.** Never invent a third item to fill a layout.
7. **Acknowledge, show action, then ask.** On a risk or delay page: the problem first, its effect on the reader (including history: "this is the second date we have moved"), the specific reason, what we are already doing, the next commitment with a date.
8. **End forward.** Close with a concrete next step: a meeting, a decision with a date, an owner. Never a summary of our capabilities.

## Visual structure

Make slides dense but easy to navigate.

- **Bolded lead-ins.** Default bullet: a 2–4 word bolded title, then one sentence. The bold text alone should give the gist.
- **Choose one of three bullet styles per list:** title only; a short 5–10 word bullet; or a bolded title plus one full sentence.
- **Two sentences maximum per bullet; three per text block.**
- **Bullets for parallel facts; numbers for decisions, asks or a real sequence.**
- **Tables** when rows and columns compare like with like.
- **Visual first.** For a sequence, flow, hierarchy, comparison or quantities, try a diagram, process flow, chart, roadmap or matrix before a text layout. Label arrows with what moves or who decides.
- **Layered disclosure.** The point and essential support in the main flow; deeper detail in a referenced appendix or detail panel.
- **Variations.** When the right form is unclear, or Ri hasn't seen this kind of page in the deck, build two to four variations (for example, diagram-led and text-dense) for Ri to choose from. Apply the choice to similar pages.

## Anecdotes

1. Use a real anecdote from the sources, memory or material in context.
2. Or ask Ri for one, saying where it would go and what it should illustrate.
3. Or write without one and note the open slot.

**Never invent an anecdote, client story, quotation or personal "I remember when…".**

## Never write

- Aphorisms that trade concrete for abstract: "Steering is the work." "It's not just a file format; it's a philosophy."
- Setup lines that only announce what comes next.
- Inflated significance: "pivotal", "fundamental challenge", "game-changing".
- "Matters more than it looks" or "more than it may initially appear".
- Vague statements about our effort or virtue: "working really hard", "fully committed", "we want to be transparent".
- Vague attribution: "experts agree", "research shows" without a source.
- Stacked hedges, or the same hedge phrase repeated across a deck.
- "That sort of thing" or any hand-wave to end a list.
- "Proven track record". Use the specific proof instead.
- Email conventions: greetings, sign-offs, "->" connectors.

## Worked slide rewrites

Facts in these examples are illustrative; never reuse them as claims.

**Title**
- Not: "Data migration: the critical path to modernization"
- Not: "Migration is the risk."
- This: "Undocumented stored procedures, not data volume, will set the migration timeline"

**Card row → bolded-lead bullets**
- Not: three cards titled "Visibility", "Governance", "Acceleration", each promising to "unlock end-to-end value".
- This:
  - **Shared intake form.** Brokers submit once, so underwriters stop re-keying submissions from email.
  - **Rules-based triage.** Submissions under the small-commercial limit go straight to that queue; the rest get a senior review.
  - **Daily aging view.** Team leads see stuck submissions each morning instead of discovering them at month-end.

**Differentiator**
- Not: "Deep payer expertise and a proven track record of delivering results for organizations like yours."
- This: Title "We stay flexible after the SOW is signed, which is where most implementations get rigid." Bullets: "**Mid-stream re-scoping.** On [two recent payer implementations], we changed scope at the client's request without a change order." "**Named continuity.** The leads who scope the work stay on it through go-live."

**Risk or delay**
- Not: "Timeline update: unforeseen complexities have emerged"
- This: Title "Go-live moves from March 14 to April 4 because the vendor's API can't return claim history yet." Body: "This is the second date we have moved, and we know that makes your planning harder." "**Why.** The vendor's claim-history endpoint fails on records older than 2022; their fix is scheduled for March 21." "**What we're doing.** We are testing against a history extract in parallel, so the vendor fix is the only remaining dependency." "**Next.** We confirm April 4 or a fallback date by Friday, March 7."

**Comparison with an asymmetry**
- Not: "Current state / Future state" columns with "Manual processes" and "Automated, intelligent workflows".
- This: Title "Your analysts spend most of close reconciling, not analyzing; the new model reverses that." Columns show hours by activity today and after, with the gap called out.

**Process diagram labels**
- Not: boxes "Intake → Review → Approval" with unlabeled arrows.
- This: arrows labeled "complete submission", "flagged exceptions only", "approved scope over $250K", plus a one-line caption stating when the process advances.

**Next steps** (numbered because each needs a decision)
1. **Choose pilot sites.** Your operations lead picks two plants by May 15.
2. **Grant system access.** Your IT team opens read access to the MES by May 22.
3. **Kick off baseline.** We start measuring on June 2.
