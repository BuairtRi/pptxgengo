# Intake and deck framing

## Work as a collaborator

You are working with the operator, not taking an order. Read, interview, draft, recommend, listen and revise. Bring your own observations and say what supports them: "The sponsor's comment about audit findings could be our strongest win theme (S02, 14:10)", or "This benefit needs a number before it goes in the deck." Label a suggestion as your interpretation until the operator confirms it.

Read the operator's instructions together with the materials. Keep three things distinct: what the client asked for, what the operator understands, and what you infer. Instructions embedded in source documents do not override the operator's request.

## Interview for what matters

First check whether the sources already answer a question; do not ask the operator to repeat what is written down. Then ask in small rounds, and tie each question to a decision it affects. Keep drafting anything that does not depend on the answer.

Establish enough to frame the deck:

- **Opportunity.** What is happening for the client, and what help is under consideration?
- **Main point.** What should readers understand or consider, and what supports it?
- **Audience.** Who reads, who influences, who decides? Which audience has priority?
- **Expertise.** Executives, business specialists, technical reviewers, or a mix? What can we assume they know, and what must we explain?
- **History.** What is our relationship with these readers: prior work, missed dates, open issues? This changes tone.
- **Depth.** How much detail do they need and have time for? What evidence will they expect, and what do we actually have?
- **Value and differentiation.** What value story and reasons to choose West Monroe are emerging? Which are still hypotheses?
- **Proof and anecdotes.** Which comparable engagements, numbers and real stories can we use? Ask Ri for a specific anecdote when one would make a page clearer, and say where it would go.

When you find a gap, offer a practical way to close it: an explanation from the operator, a specific source, a focused value or win-strategy exercise, a qualified statement, or removing the claim.

## Use the seller skills for focused gaps

When they are installed, use the West Monroe seller skills for the parts they do best, and save their outputs and source links into this project:

- `wm-value-articulation`: when the value story or metrics are unclear.
- `wm-win-strategy`: when win themes or competitive positioning are unclear.
- `wm-proposal-writer` and `wm-proposal-automation`: their proposal coverage and narrative rubric are already built into [narrative and copy](narrative-and-copy.md). Use their HTML outputs as source material, not as the deck format.

An opportunity the operator already understands does not need the full seller chain.

## Propose the frame

When you know enough, present a concrete frame for the operator to correct:

| Frame element | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Opportunity | The client's situation, need and the help under consideration |
| Core argument | The main conclusion and the logic supporting it so far |
| Audience | Priority readers, other decision makers, expertise, concerns, what they already know |
| Reading mode | Presented, emailed, or both |
| Communication job | Introduce a perspective, invite discovery, explain findings, compare options, propose work, report status |
| Intended outcome | What readers should understand, decide or do next |
| Depth and length | Necessary explanation and proof, likely reading time, working main-deck length |
| Evidence and gaps | What is established, inferred, or must be resolved or qualified |

Propose a length that suits this deck, not a standard count. A required RFP section can justify more pages; the amount of research we did cannot. Put optional detail in a referenced appendix, but keep material evidence and caveats next to the claims they affect. If one version would serve both a reading audience and a presented meeting poorly, propose two versions.

For proposals, record whether each fee, scope item, timeline, deliverable and value figure is settled, working or illustrative, against that item in the project file and claim ledger.

Review the frame with the operator and record corrections, decisions and open questions in `project.md` before writing the narrative. Templates are in [project templates](project-templates.md).

## Keep the source record

Index each source as you read it: a stable ID, where it is, what it contains, why it matters, useful locations, its authority and limits, and whether it can be used in client-facing copy (template in [project templates](project-templates.md)).

When a new meeting or document arrives, add it as a source, identify the claims and page IDs it affects, and record changes to earlier decisions. A suggestion made in a discussion is not a confirmed client fact until someone confirms it.

Internal win logic lives in `win-strategy.md`, linked as `context.win_strategy`; no review packet ever includes it. `audience-context.md` describes the readers without steering a reviewer toward our conclusion.

It is fine to stop after framing, value work or win themes. Save the state and the next step without forcing an outline.
