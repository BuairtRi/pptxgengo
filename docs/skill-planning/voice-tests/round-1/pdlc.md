# AI in your product development lifecycle: perspective deck copy

Deck type: perspective, emailed ahead of a conversation. Reader: VP of Product, [Company], eight PMs reporting. Everything the reader needs is on the page; no speaker notes.

## 1. Narrative

Your product managers spend about two days writing each PRD, while the discovery interviews that should shape those PRDs pile up as recordings nobody synthesizes. By the quarterly roadmap review, the customer evidence is out of reach, so priorities go to whoever argues hardest. The obvious use of AI is writing PRDs faster. We think the bigger gain is connecting the stages: AI synthesizes the interviews, and the same evidence then feeds the PRD, the roadmap review, and the delivery handoff, while your PMs keep the decisions at each stage. Your worry that AI will make requirements generic and PMs passive is right when AI starts the thinking, so we put the PM's choices first and make every requirement cite its interview. We'd start with discovery synthesis on one team, and we'd like to show you the result on that team's real interviews in a working session.

### P05 Starting point

- **Eyebrow:** Where we'd start
- **Title:** We'd start with discovery synthesis, because your interviews are the evidence every later stage needs
- **Numbered steps (left, a real sequence):**
  1. **Synthesize one team's interviews.** One PM shares [number] recent interview recordings; we turn them into tagged themes with source quotes.
  2. **Compare against the last PRD.** That PM checks the synthesis against the PRD they wrote in the same period: what the synthesis found that the PRD missed, and what the synthesis got wrong.
  3. **Decide whether to extend.** If the synthesis changes what the PM would have written, we add PRD drafting next. If it doesn't, we stop, and you've spent [effort estimate] finding out.
- **Panel (right), two bolded-lead bullets:**
  - **Why not PRDs first.** Drafting PRDs faster without the interview evidence produces exactly the generic requirements you're worried about.
  - **Where we've done this.** We have run AI-assisted discovery pilots with [number] product teams. [What those pilots showed: result and basis, to confirm.]
- **Visual form:** Three numbered steps down the left two-thirds; a narrow gray proof panel on the right.

## 4. Variations for P03

Both variations use the same eyebrow, title, and caption.

- **Caption (both):** Illustrative workflow. We'd tune it to your tools and test it with one team before anyone else changes how they work.

### Variation A: diagram-led flow

Four stage columns, left to right. Each has two lanes, "AI does" on top and "Your PM decides" beneath. Labeled arrows connect the stages.

| | Discovery | Definition | Prioritization | Delivery handoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AI does** | Transcribes each interview recording and tags it by theme, customer, and quote. | Drafts the PRD from the chosen themes, citing the interviews behind each requirement. | Shows how many interviews, and which customers, support each candidate item. | Drafts user stories and acceptance criteria from the PRD, linked back to the source interviews. |
| **Your PM decides** | Which themes deserve a PRD. | Scope, tradeoffs, and what to cut; signs the PRD. | You and your PMs set the ranking in the review, with the evidence on screen instead of whoever argues hardest. | Which stories are ready for the delivery team; answers the team's questions. |

Arrow labels:
- Discovery → Definition: **chosen themes with source quotes**
- Definition → Prioritization: **signed PRD, each requirement linked to its interviews**
- Prioritization → Delivery handoff: **ranked items with their interview evidence attached**

Callout under the Prioritization column: **Evidence is ready any week,** so your roadmap review doesn't have to wait for the quarter.

| Stage | What AI does | What your PM decides | What moves to the next stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Discovery** | Transcribes each interview recording and tags it by theme, customer, and quote. | Which themes deserve a PRD. | Chosen themes with source quotes |

| **Delivery handoff** | Drafts user stories and acceptance criteria from the PRD, linked back to the source interviews. | Which stories are ready for the delivery team; answers the team's questions. | Stories your delivery team can trace back to a customer |

Below the table, one bolded-lead line: **Evidence is ready any week,** so your roadmap review doesn't have to wait for the quarter.

**Tradeoff.** Variation A shows the evidence moving from stage to stage, which is the title's claim, but its four columns leave little room per label; Variation B is easier to scan for who does what but reads as a list of stages and hides the flow.

**Recommendation.** Variation A, because the page argues that the stages are connected and the arrows are what show it. Fall back to B if the four columns can't hold the labels at a readable size.
