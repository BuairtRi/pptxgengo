Executive summary

DXC is at an inflexion point with plans to become a SaaS business. The ability to make progress on a multifaceted business strategy with coordinated and coupled product and engineering teams is critical in realizing that ambition.

What we saw

What should be done

Decision path is inconsistent
Signals do not move consistently from opportunity brief to PRD, epic, technical design, and implementation plan.

Platform maturity is uneven
Reconcile AI leads on technical design and test automation; Core still relies on manual regression and has had 15 unplanned changes to-date in 2026.

Evidence arrives too late
Delivery is not measured end to end, and release validation plus post-launch learning are not yet a consistent feedback loop.

NOW
4-6 weeks

Define strategy, outcome measures, and six-month roadmaps for all products in a four-week spike. Improve quality, testing, and release readiness in parallel. Introduce lifecycle skills and templates and support usage / adoption.

NEXT
1–3 months

Execute roadmaps and use customer and business evidence to adjust priorities. Improve engineering efficiency and handoffs across the lifecycle. Develop the new business strategy in parallel. Roll updated engineering practices out to core teams.

FUTURE
January

Refresh outcomes and product roadmaps against the new business strategy in January. Execute through the upgraded lifecycle and use outcome measures to guide subsequent decisions.

Use evidence to extend what works

Use early signals to decide what becomes standard practice and what needs further iteration.

What’s needed from leadership

Name owners

One accountable owner for each product and engineering activation.

Approve pilots

Confirm pods for additional activation.

Set guardrails

Agree on the principles and ownership that will guide new delivery practices.

Drive data visibility

Jira, release, test-data, and usage access for core product areas.
