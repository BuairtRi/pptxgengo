## Speaker notes

```text
CORE MESSAGE
The business is no longer forced to reshape itself around a single software model. Organizations can make a more informed capability-by-capability choice about where off-the-shelf enterprise software should lead and where technology should be built around the business.
OFF-THE-SHELF ENTERPRISE SOFTWARE
Large enterprise platforms such as CRM and ERP bring the expertise of a product company that may spend years refining workflows, data models and operating patterns for a function or industry.
The buyer benefits from that outside expertise and avoids building the core product. The tradeoff is that the platform normally must be configured, trimmed, integrated and tailored—and the organization often adapts its people, language, processes and data to the technology selected.
BUSINESS-FIT CUSTOM SOFTWARE
A business-fit solution starts with the expertise inside the organization: the processes, practices, controls, customer experience and ways of working that have proved effective for that business.
Technology can be designed around the organization’s people and process rather than requiring the organization to adopt a vendor’s model. This does not mean preserving every current process; modernization should still improve work that creates friction or limits performance.
WHY REVISIT THE DECISION
Earlier buy-versus-build decisions were made when custom modernization required more time, specialized labor and cost. If AI-assisted analysis, prototyping, engineering, testing and maintenance materially reduce those costs, the economic threshold for a business-fit build changes.
For CRM, ERP and other large enterprise capabilities, re-evaluate prior assumptions using current implementation cost, speed to value, organizational-change burden, lifecycle operations, risk and strategic value. Do not assume that yesterday’s answer remains the right answer.
EVIDENCE POSTURE
The message is expanded buyer choice—not the demise of SaaS and not a universal preference for custom development. Off-the-shelf software remains compelling where vendor expertise, ecosystem scale, regulatory standards or common processes create value. Lower custom-build cost and lower change burden remain hypotheses to validate for each client and capability.
TRANSITION
The strategic choice becomes credible only if the underlying modernization work can now be performed at a different cost, speed and quality. The next section should demonstrate that evidence.
```
