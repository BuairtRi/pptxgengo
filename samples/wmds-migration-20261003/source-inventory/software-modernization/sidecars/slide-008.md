```text
Why complex modernization has historically been slow and expensive

Coding is only one part. The harder work is rediscovering how the business runs, deciding what should change, aligning operators and technical teams, cleaning and migrating data, validating the target, and executing a safe cutover.

DISCOVER AND DEFINE

DELIVER AND TRANSITION

01

RECONSTRUCT BUSINESS BEHAVIOR

Document current processes, rules, exceptions, and workarounds—and the business outcomes and controls they support.

02

DECIDE WHAT TO RETAIN OR REIMAGINE

Separate essential processes and controls from obsolete features, defects, duplication, and workarounds.

03

ALIGN BUSINESS AND TECHNICAL TEAMS

Translate operator intent into shared process designs, requirements, architecture, tests, training, and operating procedures.

04

UNTANGLE DEPENDENCIES AND CLEAN DATA

Map integrations and shared data, then resolve dirty, duplicate, incomplete, and inconsistent records before migration.

05

VALIDATE DURING PARTIAL CUTOVER

Run UAT while legacy and modern modules coexist, reconciling results and validating end-to-end behavior across both environments.

06

EXECUTE A SAFE CUTOVER

Sequence data and user migration, manage rollback and controls, and protect ongoing operations through the transition.
```

## Speaker notes

```text
Poor documentation and concentrated expertise make discovery slow.
Context can disappear when business intent moves into process designs, technical requirements, tests, training, and operating procedures.
Hidden dependencies cause late rework.
Validation and transition remain expensive because the business must continue operating while the system changes.
These challenges affect custom rebuilds, packaged platform changes, replatforming, and portfolio consolidation in different proportions.
Lower-cost analysis, building, and validation make a wider set of business outcomes economically credible.
```
